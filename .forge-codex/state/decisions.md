## DEC-0005 — Quarantine and reverify filesystem entries before terminal mutation

- Date: 2026-08-28
- Problem: descriptor-relative identity checks and terminal `unlinkat` or `renameatx_np` calls are separate syscalls, so a same-user process can atomically replace the final-component binding after verification.
- Evidence: `.forge-codex/architecture/SECURITY_AND_PRIVACY.md`; the four atomic-`RENAME_SWAP` matrix tests; the post-quarantine substitution test; the combined namespace/durability recovery tests; and the bounded-ledger tests named below. Current-source Debug `EVID-20260828T124332Z-1e2a050c0e` and Release `EVID-20260828T124624Z-00a3dd27c2` each pass 657 tests with four expected host-dependent skips on source manifest `bc7b2f66d381d8fa4cd51b9e4da78b8c8486572385e05425c07d94922e8c3106`.
- Constraints: preserve filesystem tool names, no-overwrite move behavior, hard-link support, cancellation accounting, bounded recursive work, and explicit recovery after an irreversible namespace change.
- Feature IDs: `MCP-TOOL-FILESYSTEM`.
- Selected option: under pinned parents, verify the requested leaf; durably reserve one of 32 global immutable receipt slots; rename the current occupant exclusively to that slot's deterministic same-parent quarantine name; reverify the path identity, plus the bounded metadata/content fence on cross-volume source cleanup; and only then unlink or publish it. Terminal mutation and rollback stay inside a scoped ledger operation, and unsafe rollback retains a fixed recovery slot.
- Ownership/lifetime impact: directory descriptors remain operation-scoped. A per-Forge-home 32-slot receipt ledger, protected by a global lock with a two-second acquisition deadline, caps Forge-created retained quarantines across cooperating processes and ordinary crashes. A terminal namespace syscall or `fsync` already executing while that lock is held has no independent wall-time deadline. Corrupt receipts, unavailable or rebound parents, unconfirmed terminal durability, and failed rollback consume their slot until recovery; no random quarantine or receipt-temporary names are created. When receipt unlink succeeds but the following ledger-directory sync fails, Forge does not claim a nonexistent live recovery path; a stale receipt may conservatively reappear after a crash.
- Compatibility impact: existing delete and move contracts remain available; adversarial replacement fails closed at the exercised pre-quarantine windows, `RENAME_EXCL` continues to prevent destination overwrite, and unconfirmed terminal durability retains a bounded receipt. Boolean presence fields widen to JSON `null` only when inspection is unknown and pair with `*_presence_known=false`; cleanup requirements remain conservative Booleans. Command-level recovery-path reporting must remain command-backed and must not be inferred merely from receipt retention.
- Resource impact: recursive plans refuse more than 100,000 entries, and cross-volume move/reconciliation is capped at 300 seconds. These caps do not bound the bytes or descendants reachable through a single substituted entry.
- Residual security impact: mitigation does not eliminate `FC-FILESYSTEM-PATH-TOCTOU-001`. An adversary that discovers and swaps the quarantine name after final verification can redirect one terminal mutation per winning race. A substituted final-link file can contain unbounded bytes; a substituted directory can contain an unbounded subtree. One recursive call can reuse released slots across as many as 100,000 planned entries, so the 32-slot accumulation bound limits simultaneous cooperative recovery state, not cumulative adversarial wrong-object mutations. It does not protect against a same-UID adversary able to alter the owner-controlled ledger or move quarantine parents. Path anchoring, destination hierarchy construction, and hard-link ctime refresh also retain documented residuals, so the finding remains open as E2.
- Tests: `testRecursiveDeletePreservesLeafSwappedAfterVerification`, `testSameVolumeMoveDoesNotPublishLeafSwappedAfterVerification`, `testCrossVolumeInstallDoesNotPublishStagingLeafSwappedAfterVerification`, and `testCrossVolumeSourceRemovalPreservesLeafSwappedAfterVerification` cover the four atomic-`RENAME_SWAP` paths. `testRollbackRefusesSubstitutedQuarantineOccupant` covers the deterministic unsafe-rollback trace. `testSameVolumeNamespaceInstabilityWithUnconfirmedDurabilityRetainsRecoveryReceipt` and `testCrossVolumeNamespaceInstabilityWithUnconfirmedDurabilityRetainsRecoveryReceipt` prove live receipt reporting when publication commits but the requested namespace is unstable and terminal durability is unconfirmed; `testCrossVolumeNamespaceInstabilityMergesInstallAndStagingRecoveryReceipts` proves that a second retained staging-cleanup receipt is merged into the result. `testDeleteQuarantineIsGloballyBoundedAndRecoveredAcrossRestart`, `testCorruptQuarantineReceiptRemainsOccupiedAndRecoveryVisible`, `testPreexistingDeterministicQuarantineNameIsNotClaimed`, `testStaleQuarantineReservationCannotReleaseReusedSlot`, `testConcurrentQuarantineReservationsNeverExceedGlobalCapacity`, and `testRestartDoesNotTreatMissingNamesAsTerminalProof` cover fixed-capacity recovery. `testInitialQuarantineSyncFailureReportsRetainedTransitionWithoutRollback`, `testDeleteReportsUnknownPresenceWithoutFalseExistenceClaim`, `testUnavailableQuarantineLedgerFailsClosedBeforeNamespaceMutation`, `testPostUnlinkReceiptSyncFailureDoesNotClaimMissingRecoveryPath`, `testReceiptRemovalFailureRetainsTerminalRecoveryPath`, `testPostPublicationStagingFailurePreservesRecoveryAndUnknownPresence`, and `testRetainedStagingRecoveryDoesNotClaimAbsentStagingPathNeedsCleanup` cover truthful failure, recovery, and wire-presence contracts. The current-source strict evidence above passes these tests; it establishes behavior at the exercised windows, not unreachability of the remaining final verifier-to-terminal-syscall window.
- Rollback: revert quarantine-and-verify only if an equivalent identity-conditional terminal mutation or independent privilege boundary replaces it; keep E2 open and rerun the complete filesystem mutation/cancellation matrix.

## DEC-0006 — Move protected filesystem mutation behind a signed LaunchDaemon

- Date: 2026-08-30
- Problem: the same-UID quarantine ledger in DEC-0005 cannot prevent its owner from altering recovery state or substituting the terminal quarantine name. The approved E2 policy forbids narrowing that threat model and keeps `FCA-007` / `FC-FILESYSTEM-PATH-TOCTOU-001` release-blocking.
- Constraints: preserve the public filesystem and shell tool contracts; keep `shell_exec` synchronous and nonprivileged; make new runtime tools additive; never fall back to same-UID mutation when the privileged capability is absent; retain explicit recovery and bounded messages.
- Selected option: ship a minimal separately signed `com.forge-conductor.filesystem-daemon` as an app-bundled LaunchDaemon registered with `SMAppService.daemon(plistName:)`. Pass an authorized project-root descriptor and bounded relative path components over an enumerated designated-requirement XPC boundary. Qualify only non-directory leaf deletion initially. The root daemon independently validates request, project generation, root and leaf identity, volume, and transaction fields; binds project and transaction state to the accepted connection's non-root effective UID; requires requester-owned ACL-free traversal and an owner-writable final parent; rejects ACL-protected or immutable/append/restricted/no-unlink leaves; exclusively captures the source into a root-owned mode-0700 same-volume namespace; persists and synchronizes each phase; reverifies the captured identity and caller-permission fence; and performs terminal unlink only inside that namespace.
- Fail-closed contract: service unavailable, approval required, peer identity mismatch, protected namespace unavailable, capability unavailable, and unqualified volume return `secure_filesystem_helper_unavailable`, `secure_filesystem_helper_not_approved`, `secure_filesystem_helper_identity_mismatch`, `protected_transaction_namespace_unavailable`, `filesystem_capability_unavailable`, and `filesystem_volume_unqualified` respectively. Directory deletion, move, cross-volume mutation, exact-content requests, and unsupported volumes have no legacy production fallback.
- Signing and lifecycle: Debug uses the local Apple Development identity for team `9AQ2C2838M`; Release retains team `2Y25RTLZET`. Hardened Runtime, nested signature validation, admin approval/denial, unregister/re-register upgrade, app and manager restart, and notarized Release behavior require signed evidence before this boundary can pass.
- API decision: descriptor-relative `openat`/`fstatat`/`unlinkat` and `renameatx_np` remain necessary but do not accept an expected identity as a mutation predicate. `RENAME_EXCL` is used to capture without overwriting, while `RENAME_SWAP` remains an attacker test primitive. `SMAppService` supplies lifecycle, not mutation identity. NSXPC uses explicit requirements for the native app and manager/CLI clients plus the exact daemon CodeDirectory hash set; production tool execution originates in the manager/CLI, so admitting only the app would be a functional denial. Apple documents that `NSXPCConnection.setCodeSigningRequirement` invalidates the connection when new messages do not match, `NSXPCListener.setConnectionCodeSigningRequirement` rejects a nonmatching peer before the delegate, and underlying XPC checks all received messages. Low-level `xpc_peer_requirement_match_received_message`, Swift XPC `XPCReceivedMessage.senderSatisfies`, and `SecCodeCreateWithXPCMessage` were evaluated but are not required merely to repeat the property already enforced by NSXPC. PID-only lookup remains insufficient and is not used.
- Residual impact: a same-UID attacker can swap the source before exclusive capture. One substituted entry can be moved temporarily into the protected namespace and may require recovery; if it is a directory, the unavailable entry can represent an unbounded subtree. Post-capture identity verification is intended to prevent terminal deletion of that substituted entry. Writable descriptors and hard links can still mutate or preserve inode content. They can also change ACL/BSD authorization metadata after the last protected-leaf check and before root `unlinkat`; the maximum terminal impact is deletion of the one captured expected regular file or symbolic link after its authorization metadata changed, and a regular file can contain unbounded bytes. Signed hostile-process evidence for NSXPC's per-message enforcement is still absent. DEC-0008 supersedes this checkpoint's missing recovery surface with bounded pathless query/resume/acknowledgement, but its signed-process qualification and automatic managed acknowledgement/discovery remain open. This is mitigation, not elimination.
- Current authority: implementation and unsigned/unit evidence are not E2 qualification. Signed wrong-client, requester-permission, full atomic-swap, crash-at-every-durable-phase, usable recovery, volume, approval/denial, signature, update/unregister, source-leaf, hard-link, and writable-FD matrices remain required. `FC-FILESYSTEM-PATH-TOCTOU-001`, E2, P10, G10, and G12 stay open.
- Rollback: disable protected mutations and keep the finding open; do not restore a same-UID production fallback.

## DEC-0007 — Bind privileged-helper identity to each running caller

- Date: 2026-08-30
- Problem: the partial service handshake hashes mutable helper paths and the daemon self-reports a pathname hash. The normal login-manager package is an ad-hoc minimal app without the helper or LaunchDaemon plist, while raw CLI callers consult caller-relative `SMAppService` state and `Bundle.main`. Those clients fail closed, and a same-team/same-identifier/same-version daemon rollback is not excluded by the current designated requirement and path hashes.
- Constraints: retain explicit app and manager/CLI identities, no team wildcard, no environment/config/argument authority for helper paths, no same-UID mutation fallback, and no claim that code signing establishes release freshness.
- Rejected option: searching for another app/helper path and statically validating it at call time. Apple's Security framework documents that static validation is valid only while the filesystem object remains unmodified, so this would remain a same-UID race and would not prove the mapped daemon image.
- Selected option: generate the allowed per-architecture daemon CodeDirectory hash set during the signed build and bind it into each main caller executable's mapped code or a kernel-attested entitlement. The client must precompile a daemon requirement conjoined with that exact `cdhash` set before activating NSXPC. The canonical app-scheme graph builds one daemon, builds and seals the CLI from that daemon, and makes the app depend on and embed both products. The app, installed manager, installed raw CLI, and standalone CLI must come from that matching artifact set, share one verified app/helper/plist relationship, and exercise it as distinct signed processes. Independently clean app and CLI builds are not treated as pairable without passing the exact seal checker. Path SHA-256 may remain diagnostic only.
- Residual impact: this prevents helper-only rollback while a current caller is running, but it does not prevent rollback of the entire validly signed caller, embedded expectation, and helper. Closing that freshness residual requires a monotonic root-owned version receipt. During any intentional multi-hash upgrade overlap, every allowlisted daemon retains its full bounded root mutation authority; the maximum possible impact includes vulnerabilities in the oldest allowlisted daemon.
- Current authority: the source implementation now seals the built daemon hash set into the signed app and CLI, composes the exact requirement before XPC activation, reports the running daemon's validated `SecCode` hash, serializes handshake/dispatch/timeout transitions, and permits raw CLI probing only with a valid sealed expectation. The app embeds the signed CLI and manager framework relationship; app-origin installation validates both callers, stages only that CLI plus the adjacent framework, and refuses incomplete, symlinked, mismatched, or invalidly signed payloads. A clean Apple Development Debug arm64 app-scheme build, exact bundle checker, and sanitized app/standalone CLI version smokes pass as supporting evidence only. Distinct installed signed app/manager/CLI XPC processes, stale-helper, wrong-signer, upgrade, restart, Release/notarization, and whole-product freshness qualification remain open. `FC-PRIVILEGED-CALLER-IDENTITY-001`, `FC-PRIVILEGED-SERVICE-LIFECYCLE-001`, `FC-FILESYSTEM-PATH-TOCTOU-001`, E2, P10, G10, and G12 remain open.
- Rollback: if the caller-sealed expectation cannot be produced for every shipped caller, leave protected mutations unavailable for those callers rather than accepting a mutable path-derived identity.

## DEC-0008 — Retain privileged terminal outcomes until exact acknowledgement

- Date: 2026-08-30
- Problem: an ambiguous `fs_delete` submission could preserve a transaction UUID but expose no path-independent query/resume operation, and terminal receipts could otherwise be recycled before a restarted caller durably consumed the result. Pathname-based retry is not valid after exclusive capture because the original name may be absent or occupied by a different object.
- Constraints: keep `fs_delete` behavior and `shell_exec` semantics intact; add recovery rather than replacing an existing tool; accept no recovery leaf path; keep all daemon authority bound to the authenticated requester, project generation, and currently authorized exact root descriptor; bound every caller and daemon ledger; never recycle an unacknowledged result.
- Selected option: protocol v4 adds exact transaction-control requests plus `queryTransaction`, `resumeTransaction`, and `acknowledgeTransaction`. The daemon retains committed, restored, and rejected outcomes in one of 32 root-owned slots until an exact acknowledgement is durably published and cleanup completes. Query is nonmutating. Resume is idempotent from the durable phase. Acknowledgement requires the exact requester UID, transaction UUID, project UUID, generation, root ID, root identity, and current root descriptor; an existing transaction with mismatched authority returns the same generic unavailable failure used by other rejected control requests. A missing transaction is an idempotently completed acknowledgement only after the supplied root descriptor and control request pass structural and root-identity validation. Automatic privileged restore through a source-parent descriptor is disabled: that directory can be relocated after validation, so rollback durably retains the captured leaf rather than risking a root write outside the authorized root.
- Crash ordering: acknowledgement metadata is written and synchronized before phase or terminal-outcome removal, and the acknowledgement marker is removed and synchronized last. A crash during cleanup therefore leaves either the complete terminal outcome or the durable marker needed to finish cleanup. Terminal slots are never age-recycled; exhaustion fails closed until explicit acknowledgement.
- Caller availability: before XPC submission, Core retains an owner-only fixed-slot record containing the original request/transaction/project/root authority and originating client for audit. Recovery from a new app or manager process reopens a root that is still in the current writable authorization scope and requires its exact recorded identity before any XPC control message. Corrupt, mismatched, unavailable, or full caller state fails closed. The caller ledger is not daemon authority; the daemon revalidates all authority independently.
- Compatibility impact: `fs_delete_recovery {transaction_id, action}` is a pathless additive MCP tool with `query`, `resume`, and `acknowledge` actions. A durable `fs_delete` terminal result now reports `acknowledgement_required=true` and remains recoverable until acknowledged. The established synchronous `/bin/bash -lc` `shell_exec` path and result contract are unchanged, and the clean-profile runtime tools remain separate nonprivileged additions.
- Residual impact: this makes bounded recovery explicit but does not add an identity predicate to `unlinkat` or `renameatx_np`. The final authorization-metadata race, writable-descriptor behavior, hard-link behavior, helper/caller whole-product rollback, binding revocation, and the unsupported directory/move/cross-volume privileged operations remain open. Normal managed execution also lacks an automatic post-broker-durable acknowledgement/discovery pass. Broker replay no longer treats pathname absence as proof that a previously existing protected leaf completed; it remains ambiguous and does not redispatch. A manager crash before the transaction UUID is delivered can nevertheless leave both bounded ledgers occupied. The caller ledger is under a same-UID-owned application directory, so a same-UID process can rename or remove the only caller recovery handle without forging daemon authority. Up to 32 such losses per protected volume can leave captured entries or terminal outcomes without normal query/resume/ack reachability and then make later mutations fail closed on capacity. Disabling automatic restore avoids the identified out-of-root rollback write but can retain one captured leaf per transaction, up to the same 32-slot volume bound. Project-generation reset holds the caller-ledger lock across both checks and generation advance, cancels on visible authority, and surfaces cancellation failure. Delete retention revalidates current project authority inside that lock, so a normal delayed request cannot publish stale authority or reach XPC. Same-UID removal or relocation of already-retained caller records can hide live daemon transactions from reset and let generation advance while old authority still completes. The same-UID writer can also replace the locked inode after a retainer validates but before slot publication, split retention from reset, and permit stale dispatch. Without daemon-owned discovery or generation revoke, maximum post-reset impact is up to 32 captured or terminally deleted expected leaves per protected volume; each regular file can contain unbounded bytes. These availability and authorization limitations are mitigation only and must not be described as E2 elimination.
- Evidence boundary: protocol, state-machine, caller-ledger, schema, authorization, and restart unit tests are supporting evidence only. The full signed distinct-process atomic-swap, wrong-client, every-durable-crash, app/manager/daemon restart, approval/update, volume, Release/notarization, and hostile hard-link/writable-descriptor matrices in `PRIVILEGED_FILESYSTEM_QUALIFICATION.md` remain required. `FC-FILESYSTEM-PATH-TOCTOU-001`, E2, P10, G10, and G12 stay open.
- Rollback: if the control plane cannot be qualified, disable protected mutation rather than removing retained outcomes, reusing unacknowledged slots, retrying from a pathname, or restoring the same-UID fallback.

## DEC-0009 — Make exclusive capture the contract-specific mutation boundary

- Date: 2026-08-30
- Problem: a source-identity comparison before `renameatx_np` is another observation and cannot make the later rename conditional on that identity. The earlier protocol also did not distinguish deletion of the entry occupying a name at execution time from deletion of one exact namespace version or one exact content version.
- Constraints: retain the signed root daemon and protected same-volume namespace; keep `fs_delete` and pathless recovery behavior compatible; keep `shell_exec` synchronous and nonprivileged; add no same-UID fallback; preserve protocol-v4 crash recovery; and keep E2, P10, G10, and G12 open until their signed evidence exists.
- Selected option: protocol v5 defines `currentEntry`, `namespaceVersionExact`, and `contentVersionExact`. It binds request, transaction, project generation, root, relative components, access, contract, and expected identity where applicable into a canonical SHA-256 digest. A successful `renameatx_np(..., RENAME_EXCL)` into the helper-owned slot is the linearization point. Current-entry may dispose the eligible captured occupant. Namespace-exact may dispose only a captured identity matching the request token; a mismatch becomes exclusive restore where proven safe or durable protected quarantine. Content-exact fails closed with `filesystem_content_exclusivity_unavailable` without a qualified exclusive-writer proof.
- Recovery and replay: v5 phase records use schema 3, persist protocol and digest-canonicalization versions, and are accepted only when reconstruction reproduces their canonical digest. The only migrated protocol-v4 shape is schema 2 with nil protocol, canonicalization, contract, and digest plus an expected identity, interpreted as namespace-exact. A legacy rollback reports restored only after the source has the exact recorded identity. An absent v5 protected capture or unprovable legacy restore publishes a durable conflicted outcome rather than inferring restoration. Capture identity and phase receipts use different durable names, valid transaction-bound pending identity receipts are adopted before cleanup, and post-capture recovery operates from the protected slot without reopening the user-controlled source parent. Documented atomic-rename failures that leave both names unchanged publish a durable rejected outcome; EEXIST, I/O ambiguity, descriptor corruption, and unknown failures retain recovery.
- API decision: descriptor-relative `openat`, `fstat`, `fstatat`, `lstat`, `openat_authenticated_np`, `unlinkat`, `renameat`, and `renameatx_np` were evaluated. `RENAME_EXCL` provides an atomic no-overwrite capture and `RENAME_SWAP` provides the adversarial substitution primitive, but no public operation accepts an expected device, inode, vnode generation, or content token as the predicate for the terminal mutation. The equivalent boundary is capture into a root-owned namespace followed by contract-specific verification; this is a mitigation checkpoint until its formal argument and signed evidence pass.
- Evidence contract: the canonical 57-row report is structured per case and records every exercised contract, raw artifacts, iteration/barrier facts, process and signing identities, before/after fixture digests, mount facts, crash points, and observed outcome. Boolean-only matrices are invalid. The template records `partial`, `ok=false`, and `not_run`; it is not pass evidence.
- Residual impact: terminal quarantine currently has no separately authorized restore, release, or purge operation and cannot be acknowledged while the captured entry exists. Each conflict permanently consumes one of 32 transaction slots; 32 conflicts disable later protected deletion. The bound is entries, not bytes or descendants: a pre-capture substitution can quarantine a directory containing an unbounded subtree. The daemon also lacks a startup recovery/fencing barrier for unrelated nonterminal transactions. Terminal receipts do not yet have a qualified repair transition when their physical protected-leaf disposition conflicts with the recorded terminal state. The final ACL/BSD-metadata-check-to-`unlinkat` race can delete one already captured eligible regular file or symlink after its authorization metadata changes, and a regular file can contain unbounded bytes. Writable descriptors and hard links prevent immutable-content claims. Caller-ledger tamper/generation fencing, binding revoke/garbage collection, behavior-qualified external/removable volumes, whole-product rollback freshness, and every required signed adversarial/crash/lifecycle row remain open. These are mitigation residuals, not elimination.
- Parent-containment residual: source-parent validation and capture are not one
  atomic authorization operation. A same-UID process can relocate the pinned
  validated parent outside the configured root before capture. One winning
  race can delete one eligible outside-root regular file or symlink under
  `currentEntry`, or namespace-exact with a matching token; that file can
  contain unbounded bytes. A captured ineligible directory can quarantine an
  unbounded subtree. Root-relative `RENAME_RESOLVE_BENEATH` does not by itself
  bind the rename-selected parent to the separately observed requester
  ownership, ACL, write authority, and durability handle. Outside-root sentinel
  qualification and formal closure therefore remain nonpassing.
- Compatibility impact: production `fs_delete` selects `currentEntry`; the pathless recovery tool remains additive. Existing shell policy, MCP registration, synchronous `/bin/bash -lc` execution, result contract, restart behavior, and clean-profile runtime tools are not routed through the privileged daemon and are not replaced.
- Current authority: focused protocol, client, coordinator, and source-guard tests are supporting evidence only. There is no direct daemon test target, signed installed-host atomic-swap/crash matrix, notarized Release qualification, quarantine disposition, or startup recovery fence. `FC-FILESYSTEM-PATH-TOCTOU-001` / `FCA-007`, E2, P10, G10, and G12 remain mandatory release blockers.
- Rollback: disable the protected mutation operation if protocol-v5 capture, recovery, or evidence controls cannot be preserved; do not reintroduce a precompare-and-mutate or same-UID fallback.

## DEC-0010 — Bind each privileged-filesystem claim to one bounded semantic snapshot

- Date: 2026-08-30
- Problem: provenance, hashes, and a structured report do not prevent one generic artifact from being reused as support for unrelated cases, roles, iterations, or formal predicates.
- Constraints: preserve the 57-row qualification authority, keep every unexecuted row and formal predicate explicitly nonpassing, accept only current-manifest recorder-preserved read-only snapshots, and make envelopes producible by the real signed child without allowing inherited context to select an evidence identity.
- Selected option: report schema v2 uses artifact-binding schema v1. Each JSON envelope binds its qualification, recorder-assigned evidence identifier, current source manifest, exact case/role/iteration/subject or formal predicate, and the canonical SHA-256 digest of the report fact. A dedicated qualification-context envelope binds the exact capture timestamp, repository, test environment, process identities, and same-UID declarations. Before launch, the recorder captures a bounded versioned context, removes inherited evidence-context keys, passes the exact context to the child, and reproduces it in the read-only evidence record. The checker requires the record, envelope, and report to agree exactly.
- Identity and provenance: a passed report requires a nonempty active branch, 40-hex execution HEAD, canonical base `main`, the exact SHA from required `refs/remotes/origin/main`, canonical repository path, nonempty macOS build, hardware model, platform, architecture, and a context reference. The live base must match and be an ancestor of the execution HEAD; the execution HEAD must resolve and remain an ancestor of current HEAD. A bounded commit-history path scan permits intervening commits only under `.forge-codex/state/**` and `.forge-codex/evidence/**`; even a later-reverted product, script, or arbitrary repository path fails. Source-manifest targets must be clean in the worktree. Semantic envelopes must be evidence-ID-specific recorder-preserved repository copies; external hash-only artifacts and stdout/stderr streams are rejected. Every semantic evidence record requires bounded, nonplaceholder, ordered ISO-8601 timestamps plus exact build, hardware, platform, architecture, and repository-cwd provenance. The report capture timestamp must fall inside the context record's interval.
- Bounded loading: the checker performs strict canonical repository-relative validation, opens every intermediate and final component descriptor-relatively without following symlinks, and reads at most 1 MiB from the final descriptor. The object must be a regular current-euid-owned, singly linked, mode-`0444` file with the recorded byte count. Pre/post-read device, inode, type/mode, owner, link count, size, modification time, and change time must match; the digest must match; and a second no-follow lookup must still name the same regular file. Mutation during the read and pathname replacement before the second lookup are rejected. This verifies a bounded read-only snapshot at evaluation time, not broader filesystem immutability.
- Rejected evidence: missing or mismatched qualification context; null, stale, or self-inconsistent repository and host identity; external or stream semantic storage; incomplete, reversed, obsolete, or mismatched timing and environment provenance; cross-case or cross-role reuse; duplicate formal-predicate support; mismatched recorder context; stale source manifests; placeholder claims or scope; mismatched fact digests; post-reference byte mutation; and read-time mutation are nonpassing.
- Authority boundary: these controls prove only that, under a trusted operator and a quiescent same-UID writer during manifest capture, recording, and evaluation, the recorder preserved a semantically scoped child snapshot and the report cites its exact bytes. Mode `0444` and current-euid ownership are not authentication: a same-UID process can author or replace a record or snapshot before evaluation and can replace it after the check. The recorder executes an operator-selected command and does not authenticate an arbitrary harness or prove its claim. Aggregate report/evidence-record reads remain outside the 1 MiB semantic-copy bound, and direct G10 gate-runner integration remains deferred. The controls do not execute a signed distinct-process case, close a formal predicate, establish an identity-conditional macOS mutation, or eliminate any filesystem residual.
